The Genesis of Sales — Trainer Notes

9 slides · narration script + elaboration cues

How to use this document

Slide 01

Introduction — Opening the Session

Setting the foundation

Narration (what they hear)

Welcome to the team. Before we dive into the deep-rooted psychological dynamics of sales, let's establish our foundation. This session is designed to fearlessly explore the evolution of persuasion — from ancient narratives, all the way to the modern AI-first environments we operate in today. As the saying goes: great things in business are never done by one person. They are done by a team of people. And that is exactly what this session is about: aligning all of us, as one team, on the timeless mechanics of how human beings buy.

Riff on these three points

  1. This is mindset training, not product training. Make it clear that nothing in this session is about Holiday.com features or pricing — it's about rewiring how each presales person thinks about persuasion before they ever open their laptop on a discovery call.
  2. Persuasion has always been the same skill. The medium changes — fire-side stories, town criers, Mad Men ads, LinkedIn DMs, AI-generated outbound — but the underlying psychology of how a human decides to say yes hasn't moved an inch in 4,000 years. Anchor the audience: master the psychology and every new tool becomes a multiplier instead of a crutch.
  3. "One team" is a commitment, not a slogan. Call out that everyone in the room — AEs, SEs, SDRs, leadership — handles the same prospect's psychology at different points in the funnel. A misaligned story across roles is the #1 reason deals stall. Today is about a shared mental model.
Slide 02

What is $ales?

Provocation / discussion

Narration (what they hear)

So let's start with the most basic question of all. What is sales? Take a moment and really sit with it. You will notice the dollar sign hidden inside the word, and that's the trap. Most people see sales as transactions. As money changing hands. But the answer goes far, far deeper than that. Let's open it up.

Riff on these three points

  1. Make them say it out loud. This is the only slide where you should physically pause. Ask 2–3 people in the room "in one sentence — what is sales?" Capture the variety. You'll get everything from "closing deals" to "helping people." That spread is the lesson: we don't have a shared definition, and yet we expect ourselves to execute as one team.
  2. The dollar-sign trap is real. When a presales person sees sales as money-in, they start optimising for the close. When they see it as value exchange, they start optimising for the customer's transformation — and revenue follows. Reps who carry the dollar-sign mindset hit quota; reps who carry the value-exchange mindset get promoted.
  3. Tease the next slide deliberately. Say something like "Hold your definition in your head — I want to see how close you got." Builds anticipation and rewards the room for engaging with the question instead of zoning out.
Slide 03

What Really Is Sales?

Defining the discipline

Narration (what they hear)

Sales is not just a transaction. It is the art, and the science, of human persuasion, and the exchange of value. It is about identifying a need, reframing perspectives, and enabling a transformation, from a current state, to a desired future state. At its core, every sales interaction is a psychological bridge between a problem and a solution, built on trust and strategic communication. That is the discipline we are really practicing every single day.

Riff on these three points

  1. Art AND science — not one or the other. The "art" is the empathy, the framing, the timing of a pause on a call. The "science" is the repeatable methodology: discovery framework, MEDDIC, qualification gates, forecast hygiene. Reps who lean only on art are inconsistent; reps who lean only on science are robotic. We hire for both.
  2. "From current state to desired future state" is the only sentence that matters. If a rep can't articulate the prospect's current state and desired future state in one breath, they don't have a deal — they have a feature demo waiting to die. Encourage everyone to write this on the inside cover of their notebook.
  3. Trust is the rate-limiter on every deal. Walk through the equation: speed of close = depth of trust ÷ size of perceived risk. You cannot push a number up; you can only build trust faster or shrink perceived risk. That reframes every objection-handling moment going forward.
Slide 04

Disclaimer

Perspective & agnostic intent

Narration (what they hear)

A quick disclaimer before we go deeper. The biblical references I am about to use are not intended to attack or degrade any religious group, culture, or belief. From an agnostic viewpoint, these references serve as a fearless anchoring of glaring narrative facts found within an ancient written text that has existed for millennia. The goal is simple: to explore the deep-rooted psychological dynamics of the human mind, and what may very well be the first recorded sales transaction in history.

Riff on these three points

  1. Say this slowly and look around the room. Don't rush it. Acknowledge that the next four slides use Eden as a vehicle, not a doctrine. If anyone is visibly uncomfortable, offer them the room to opt out without judgement. Respect earns you permission to teach.
  2. Why this story and not another? Mesopotamian markets, Phoenician traders, the Silk Road — all great examples, but they're transactional. Eden is the only documented narrative we have where you can see the psychology of persuasion dissected step by step. That's why it's our case study, regardless of faith.
  3. Set the analytical frame. Tell them: from this point on, we're not reading scripture, we're reading a sales transcript. Anything I describe is a behaviour you'll see on a Zoom call this quarter. The costumes change; the choreography doesn't.
Slide 05

The First "Close"

The masterclass in persuasion

Narration (what they hear)

While historians look to Mesopotamian markets, the Silk Road, and so on, the true black and white origin of sales traces back to a garden. And a masterclass in persuasion. The setting: The Garden of Eden. The salesperson: The Serpent. The prospect: Eve. And the product: the Apple, which represents knowledge. The first ever closed-won deal happened in the Garden of Eden, when the serpent pitched the world to Eve.

Riff on these three points

  1. The Serpent did discovery first. Most reps skip this. The Serpent didn't open with "buy my apple" — he opened with a question: "Did God really say…?" That's textbook open-ended discovery. He surfaced an assumption before he ever proposed a solution. Drive home: no discovery, no deal.
  2. Eve was already qualified before the pitch. She had budget (free will), authority (decision-maker in her own right), and a latent need (curiosity). The Serpent didn't create demand — he uncovered it. Most lost deals in our pipeline today are reps trying to manufacture a need in an unqualified account.
  3. The product was knowledge — not the apple. The apple is the SKU; the transformation is the value. Ask the room: "What does Holiday.com actually sell? Trips? Or the freedom to disappear?" Anchor that every product they sell has a tangible shell and an emotional core, and the close happens on the emotional core.
Slide 06

Anatomy of the Original Pitch

The four moves

Narration (what they hear)

Now let's break down the anatomy of that original pitch. The Serpent did not just talk. He used the core principles that drive every modern sales funnel today. One. He identified the pain point: the gap between where Eve was, and where she could be. Two. He handled the objection, reframing risk as opportunity. Three. He delivered a value proposition focused on the return on investment of transformation, not the cost of the transaction. And four. He created urgency, triggering the first recorded instance of FOMO. Does that sound familiar?

Riff on these three points

  1. Map each move to a Holiday.com motion. Pain point → discovery call. Objection reframe → security/compliance pushback. ROI of transformation → business-case slide in the proposal. FOMO → "your competitor X just signed last quarter." Make it concrete; the abstraction only lands when it's tied to something they did this week.
  2. The objection reframe is the highest-leverage skill in the entire deck. "You will surely not die" is a one-sentence reframe that flipped existential risk into upside. Challenge them: next time a prospect says "we're not ready," your job is not to retreat — it's to reframe what "ready" means. Run a 60-second role-play in the room if time allows.
  3. FOMO is not a tactic — it is a psychological constant. Loss aversion is roughly twice as powerful as the equivalent gain (Kahneman). When you frame a deal as "what you'll lose by waiting" instead of "what you'll gain by buying," you're not being slimy — you're being accurate about how human brains actually weigh decisions. Use it ethically; pretending it doesn't exist is naive.
Slide 07

From Eden to Boardrooms

Sales core principles

Narration (what they hear)

To understand sales today, is to understand the Apple and the Serpent as metaphors for the buyer–seller dynamic. One. The Apple is the solution. Every product you sell is an Apple. It represents the promise of a new better — a bridge to a desired state of being. Two. The Serpent is the consultant. The catalyst. The one who moves a prospect from status quo, to action, through narrative. And three. The result is the exchange. Eve traded security for an upgrade. And this reveals the most important truth in sales: value is subjective. Therefore, value is perceived.

Riff on these three points

  1. "Catalyst, not pusher." A pusher tries to drag a prospect across the line. A catalyst lowers the activation energy until the prospect crosses it on their own. The first feels like sales; the second feels like leadership. Our presales team should be coached toward the second — every single call.
  2. Status quo is the real competitor. Statistically, 60% of qualified pipeline is lost to "no decision" — not to a rival vendor. Eve's competitor wasn't another salesperson; it was the comfort of doing nothing. Train the team to identify the cost of inaction in every discovery, because that's the real deal they're fighting.
  3. "Value is perceived" — pricing implications. If value is subjective, then identical pricing produces opposite outcomes on two different accounts. A $50K contract is a bargain to a prospect who sees a $500K opportunity and an outrage to one who sees a $30K problem. Our job in presales isn't to defend price — it's to expand perception of value before price is ever shown.
Slide 08

Eternal Psychology

Closing thought about the fall

Narration (what they hear)

So here is the closing thought. Eve's encounter with the serpent and the fruit, in the Garden of Eden, teaches us that sales is not merely an economic activity. It is a deeply rooted human psychological encounter. It is the process of painting a vision so compelling, that the prospect is willing to cross a threshold they previously feared. The tools have changed since the beginning of time. But the essential psychology of closing a sale remains eternal.

Riff on these three points

  1. Fear is always present in a closing moment — even on a "no-brainer" deal. Signing a contract is the prospect making a public commitment to change. Change is loss of the known. Acknowledge their fear before you ask for the close, and you'll see signature rates climb. Most reps try to drown the fear in features; the best reps name it and walk through it with them.
  2. The vision-painting muscle is trainable. Most reps describe what the product does. Top reps describe what the prospect's Tuesday morning looks like after implementation. Specificity is the entire skill. Drill it in 1:1s — pick a real opportunity and have the rep narrate the prospect's first month of life post-deal in 90 seconds.
  3. AI is a tool, not a substitute for this psychology. Bring it home: every AI-first feature we ship — automated outreach, agentic workflows, generative briefs — only scales presales if the underlying psychology is mastered. AI amplifies what the rep brings; if the rep brings transactional thinking, AI scales transactional thinking. Mindset is the leverage point.
Slide 09

Thank You

The journey ahead

Narration (what they hear)

The first close redefined our history. But your next close defines your future. May today's introduction and mind session fuel our visions of a future worth crossing the threshold for. Thank you, team.

Riff on these three points

  1. Hand them a one-thing takeaway. Ask each person to write down — physically, not in their head — one principle from today they will use on the very next call they take. The cognitive cost of writing it down dramatically increases the chance it actually happens. This is the close of our sale to them.
  2. Frame this as Day One, not a one-off. Tell them the rest of the training arc — discovery deep-dive, objection clinics, demo storytelling, negotiation mechanics — all sits on top of today's foundation. If this lesson doesn't land, the rest is unstable scaffolding. Today they bought the philosophy; the technique modules are coming.
  3. Open the floor — and stay open. End with a deliberate 5-minute open-mic for questions and pushback. Some of the strongest learnings emerge from a presales rep saying "but in my industry…" Reward the dissent publicly. It signals that mindset training is a dialogue, not a download.
